## What the tests exercise

1. **Desktop WebGPU:** light movement; pool touch ripples; three camera views and reset; drop initiation, falling phase and impact; repeat activation by a real raycast hit on the leaf; snail close-up, retraction and re-extension; audio state; modal open/close; zoom and reset.
2. **Touch:** a tap on the leaf itself, touch drag, two-finger pinch, no viewport overflow, and portrait-to-landscape resize.

4. **Reduced motion / keyboard:** preference detection, arrow-key light control, light reactivation, Enter on the drop button, and dialog access.
5. **Unavailable renderer:** an actionable readable recovery panel appears.

## Findings fixed during visual QA

- WebGPU rejected a shared transmission texture when a smaller planar reflection target changed its dimensions. Matching the reflector resolution to the main scene removed those GPU validation errors.
- The initial scene camera needed positioning before the first render.
- An offscreen label could enlarge the mobile layout viewport, shifting the canvas and touch coordinates. A clipped fixed viewport, container-based renderer sizing, and a clamped label fixed this. The test asserts the actual 390 px canvas width.
- Bark and fern materials were too glossy; roughness, geometry and highlights were refined. Depth of field separates the clearing from the background.
- The second material pass replaced the remaining uniformly smooth surfaces with scanned bark/ground detail, patchy procedural relief, roughness maps, cached directional shadows, and irregular organic silhouettes.
- The original hanging drop was always visible and the snail crossed the water. A hidden tear-shaped reservoir, longer slide phase, stronger leaf sway, layered water normals, and a shoreline placement corrected those cues.
- A frond behind the hero droplet gives its refraction visible detail to enlarge/distort.
- The snail now uses a less obstructed close-up and a properly tapered tail.

- Parallax pass (2026-09-21): the desktop camera barely responded to the pointer - the left and right extremes were near-identical frames - so the swing was widened roughly fourfold with the look target following a fraction of it. Both extremes were reviewed in the wide, waterline and leaf-study views: the hero leaf, pool and headline all stay framed, and the foreground now travels visibly against the log and the trunks. Touch viewports are unchanged.
- Movement pass (2026-09-21): wheel input was normalised across delta modes after finding that a line-reporting browser moved the zoom about 33x less than a pixel-reporting one for the same physical notch; `+` / `-` were added because looking closer had no keyboard path; and the camera follow was rebuilt, having previously smoothed an already-smoothed value at half rate for roughly three quarters of a second of lag. Measured settling for three notches: 0.35 immediately, 0.45 by ~600 ms, with pixel, line and keyboard input landing within 0.01 of each other. Two tests were added (**6 passed**); the existing desktop test caught an over-tight per-event clamp during this pass, which was corrected.

- Falling drop pass (2026-09-21): the bead more than doubled in size the instant it left the leaf (0.68 against the 0.32 it carried while sliding), fell as a rigid pendant with its neck and point still built into the lathe, and stopped at an arbitrary zero rather than the waterline. It now inherits its size from the leaf, is a volume-matched sphere once airborne, leaves elongated and rings down as surface tension settles it, and lands where its underside meets the water. The fall was shortened from 0.90 s to 0.72 s. Sampled frame by frame from detach to impact via a new `dropFalling` / `dropFallPosition` diagnostic; ripples still originate from the actual landing point.

- Sliding drop pass (2026-09-21): measured against the leaf, the running bead was 0.093 wide by 0.251 tall - a gumdrop standing on end rather than water on a surface - and the lobe trailing it was 0.199 wide, more than twice the bead it was supposed to neck back to. It is now a flattened dome, 0.196 by 0.088 by 0.240 along its travel, sunk slightly into the blade so it wets rather than rests on it, lying along the local slope instead of tumbling, with the neck narrower than the bead. The falling radius is derived from that volume rather than a hand-tuned constant, so the two stay consistent if either is retuned. A first attempt sized the dome too small to read as the hero event and was corrected. Sampled across the slide in the leaf-study view.

- Planting pass (2026-09-21): the moss carpet was setting a per-instance colour on every one of its 28,000 blades and then discarding it, because assigning `colorNode` replaces the diffuse chain that instance colour multiplies into; the base-to-tip darkening now rides in vertex colours so both survive. Foliage gained a translucency term and a water film in the clearcoat. The film was first set tight enough to blow out into mirror highlights on the broad leaves and was softened and broken up with a roughness map; the moss carries less of it to avoid specular aliasing on geometry that small. Reviewed in the wide and waterline views on WebGPU and forced WebGL 2 and at 390x844; frame counts and draw calls were unchanged.

- Atmosphere and frame-rate pass (2026-09-21): ground mist was added, then rebalanced twice - first too faint to register, then a uniform wash veiling the pool - before being made patchy, low-lying and weighted behind the water. The broad understory leaves turned out never to have received the translucency added in the previous pass: `leafMat.emissiveNode` was assigned twice and the second assignment silently replaced it. The wet film was made patchy rather than uniform, which was the plastic tell. Measured frame rate at 1512x940, five trials, median: 47 fps before the planting pass, 29 fps after it, 26 fps with mist on top. The cause was the clearcoat added to the moss carpet, not the mist; removing it returned the scene to a vsync-capped 60 fps with mist and everything else still on. Re-measured at 60 fps median across five trials, with 6 tests passing on both backends.

- Dew and pool pass (2026-09-21), including a correction to the previous entry. Dew beads were added across the carpet, and the pool was given bed detail read through the water plus a broad moon sheen, after the unlit pool was found to be a smooth tone ramp with no structure. A first attempt at a tight specular sparkle on the water did nothing: from these camera angles the reflected ray only comes within about 0.78 of the moon direction, and 0.78^210 is zero. A first chop band moired into visible stripes and was rebuilt on unrelated, non-axis-aligned frequencies.
- **Correction:** the frame-rate figures in the previous entry were measured wrongly. The benchmark sampled its window immediately after `ready`, so it was counting shader compilation, not steady-state rendering. Re-measured with a 4 s warm-up, five trials: the scene holds a vsync-capped 60 fps with the moss clearcoat on, with 1,100 dew beads, and with everything in this pass enabled. The moss clearcoat did not cost half the frame rate; it is still left off, but for the visual reason (aliasing on blades that small), not a performance one. What the original numbers did show is real but different: added material variants lengthen first-load shader compilation, during which the opening seconds are janky. That is worth watching, and is not what the earlier note claimed.

- Navigation and copy pass (2026-09-21): **Let it rain** was renamed **Release the drop** throughout, since one bead is not rain; the field note was reworded to match. Looking around was added on drag, which was unused input on a mouse because the light follows the bare pointer there; two travelling fingers do the same on touch, where one finger stays with the light. Two tests were added covering that a drag swings the view while leaving the light where it was, that Escape restores it, and that a click which does not drag still reaches the scene. **8 passed**, 60 fps median across five warmed-up trials, both backends clean.

- Cursor and ripple pass (2026-09-21): the drawn ring around the carried light was removed - an outline around a light in a forest reads as interface rather than as a light - leaving a soft glow that swells over anything activatable, so the hover affordance survives. Ripple wavefronts were measured as plain radius, which makes a mathematically perfect circle; the radius is now warped by a smooth function of direction, built from the offset's own unit-vector components so no angle is needed. The travelling rings were likewise perfect and only 2% wide, reading as drawn strokes; they now carry irregular outlines and fade to nothing at both edges through vertex colour, so they read as crests. Checked at 1512x940 on both backends, 8 tests passing, 60 fps median.

- Ripple rework (2026-09-21): the travelling rings were drawn ring meshes laid over the water, expanding at 0.62-0.70 units/s while the shader wavefront underneath them travelled at 0.90-0.95 - two unsynchronised sets of ripples at once, and the drawn ones read as strokes rather than as water. The meshes are gone and the surface now carries the whole event. Removing them first made the ripple nearly invisible: normal perturbation alone has little to modulate over the middle of the pool, where the bed is too deep to read and the reflection is weak at that angle. The wave now also bends the bed, distorts the reflection, and shades the surface directly. Confirmed the tap was registering throughout by reading `touchAge` from the diagnostic state rather than guessing from the picture. **8 passed**; frame rate measured at a median 60 fps across three separate five-trial runs after one run returned 47.8 under load from the test suite.

- Touch ripple pass (2026-09-21): the tap was carrying nearly the weight of the falling drop's impact and arriving at full amplitude on its first frame, so it read as a shove rather than a touch. It now uses a tighter, slower packet with a finer wavelength and rises over roughly the first tenth of a second. A first attempt cut the amplitude to a quarter and left the touch invisible by 0.4 s, which is worse than too strong; it was rebalanced to about half and checked frame by frame at 60, 140, 260 and 420 ms. The drop impact is unchanged and still reads as the heavier event.

- Full review pass (2026-09-21). Found and fixed: the headline was sized from viewport width alone (`clamp(90px, 9.8vw, 155px)`) with no height rule anywhere in the stylesheet, so on a short window it kept its full height and ran down into the footer, colliding with the location caption - reproduced at 1440x620. A first fix moved the block up and introduced a second collision with the header at 1024x560 and 844x390, so the header's height is now the floor. `<h1>After<br>Rain.</h1>` had no space across the break and was announced as "AfterRain."; the decorative `01`/`02`/`03` numerals ran into their labels, so the buttons were announced as "01Follow the light". Both are now correct, verified against exact accessible names rather than `textContent`. One import was left dead by the ripple rework; `noUnusedLocals` and `noUnusedParameters` are now on so that cannot accumulate silently. A layout-collision test across five window shapes was added: **9 passed**.

- Snail surfacing (2026-09-21): the shell and skin were the last fully matte surfaces in a scene that is otherwise wet, both at `clearcoat: 0`. They now carry a water film in the clearcoat like the foliage, broken up by their own roughness maps. Frame-rate note: absolute readings in this session drifted downward over time - the unchanged commit measured 60 fps median earlier in the day and 44.8 later - so the change was judged by a back-to-back A/B in one window rather than against an earlier figure: 42.4 with the film against 44.8 without, roughly 5 per cent for a small object, which is accepted. Treat single absolute fps numbers taken late in a long session as unreliable.

- Firefly and shell pass (2026-09-21), both prompted by the owner's eye rather than by a check. The firefly's wing was a single rigid plate spanning four times the body's length, run through the insect on a glossy material, so it caught a specular highlight and read as a white rod with a light on it; replaced by two small matte translucent wings beating in opposition, with the light now pulsing on a shaped curve. The snail's shell, glossed in the previous pass, read as lacquered ceramic at close range - hard white streaks over a uniformly glossy dome. The film is now weak with a broad highlight, and the growth striations read again. Both checked at deviceScaleFactor 3-4 on the close-up. `fireflyScreen` was added to the diagnostic state so the insect can be framed precisely for review. **9 passed**, 60 fps median, both backends clean.

- Hero leaf pass (2026-09-21). At deviceScaleFactor 3 the blade read as a flat pale sheet with its veins painted on and its beads milky. Three causes: the colour map's bezier veins and the shared surface map's straight ridges were generated independently, so relief never coincided with a visible vein - the vein network is now drawn once and used for both; the leaf tint washed the painted texture out and was deepened; and the resting beads shared the hero drop's water at `thickness: 0.62` against their own ~0.05 diameter. Bead sizes now follow a heavy-tailed spread weighted toward the cup and midrib. Frame rate judged by back-to-back A/B rather than an absolute reading: 50.8 fps with the change against 47.2 without, so no cost. Absolute figures remain depressed late in a long session - see the note above.

- Release transition pass (2026-09-21): measured across the slide-to-fall handover, the bead's height tripled and its width halved on a single frame, because the two phases each described the shape independently. A 0.5 s hang was added between them; the fall now starts from the shape handed over rather than re-deriving it from a radius, and the wet streak dries back over the fall instead of vanishing on the detach frame. Two faults were found while checking it: the hang first lowered the drop beneath the blade, where it was hidden behind the leaf from a camera that reads the surface from above, and the pendant proportion inherited from the old fall code (1.85 tall to 0.72 wide) made a capsule rather than a bead. The hero water also carried `thickness: 0.62` against its own ~0.08 diameter - the same over-attenuation already corrected on the resting beads - and came out grey. Verified frame by frame through `dropSliding`, `dropSlideScale` and `dropSlideScreen`, added to the diagnostic state for this purpose.
- Noted, not fixed: one test run in this session failed two cases and logged a WebGPU `GPUValidationError` ("invalid due to a previous error") during the viewport-resize test. Six consecutive resizes in isolation produced zero console errors and the backend stayed on WebGPU, and two further full runs passed 9/9, so this looks like a cascade from context teardown in the harness rather than a fault in the resize path. It has not been reproduced and is not claimed fixed.

- Deployment (2026-09-21): published to GitHub Pages from `main` by Actions. A Pages project site serves from a subpath, which the build could not have survived unchanged - textures were requested from `/textures/...` and the favicon from `/favicon.svg`, both resolving above the site, so the scene would have loaded with no bark or ground texture and no icon. Caught before deploying by serving the built output under a subpath locally rather than assuming. The live site was then checked on three paths: desktop WebGPU, forced WebGL 2 and 390x844 portrait, each reporting ready with zero failed requests and zero console errors.
- Licensing (2026-09-21): the repository was already public with no LICENSE while the README and CREDITS described the source as private. `LICENSE` now states all rights reserved and both documents were corrected. Third-party attribution needed no change: CREDITS.md, `public/licenses/` and `public/textures/sources.json` were already complete, with notices copied into the build.

- First real-device report (2026-09-21): the owner opened the deployed site on a phone in Safari and Chrome and found it slow to start and stuttery at first. Measured rather than guessed, under six-times CPU throttling: 4.2 s to ready, of which `firstFrame` - the first `pipeline.render()`, where the scene's forty-odd node materials compile - was 2.7 s. Startup phase timings were added to `state()` so this can be attributed instead of estimated. `compileAsync` before the first frame was tried and rejected: it compiles much of the chain twice and measured 5.4 s against 4.8 s. Two fixes landed instead. The loading screen was completely static, so a blocked main thread looked identical to a hang; its animation is transform and opacity only, which run on the compositor and keep moving while the thread is busy. And the photographic payload, which must finish downloading before the first frame, went from 3.24 MB to 1.5 MB. Time to ready fell to 3.7 s throttled. On the mobile tier runtime was already fine on this machine - 2.6 M triangles against 6.2 M on desktop, holding 60 fps at four-times throttling - but that says nothing about a real phone GPU, which remains unverified.

- Performance review (2026-09-21). Measured with vsync disabled so the numbers are real render cost rather than the refresh cap, at retina scale (the renderer caps the pixel ratio at 1.65, so DPR 2 and DPR 3 render identically): 1440x900 88 fps, 1512x940 79 fps, 1920x1080 65 fps, 2560x1440 44 fps. So there is comfortable headroom on a laptop panel and none on a large external display. Cost attribution at 2560x1440: depth of field 5.8 ms (24% of the frame), planar reflector 5.8 ms (24%), bloom 2.8 ms (12%) - roughly half the frame in the first two, both already disabled on the mobile tier. The mobile tier itself holds 60 fps at six-times CPU throttling with 2.05 M triangles against 6.22 M on desktop, though that says nothing about a real phone GPU.
- The adaptive quality step will not catch the large-display case. It samples frames 180-360, counts those slower than 29 ms, and acts only if more than 95 of 180 qualify - a threshold of roughly 34 fps. At 2560x1440 the scene sits at 44 fps, comfortably above it, so no reduction ever fires and the frame rate stays below the refresh rate indefinitely. It also only lowers the pixel ratio, never the two effects that actually dominate.
- Measurement caution, learned twice in this session: absolute frame rates from this machine are not trustworthy late in a long run. Three consecutive identical trials gave 45, 46 and 60 fps. Worse, a first attempt at cost attribution was run while vsync-capped at 60, where disabling work cannot show a saving and every feature therefore appeared free. Attribute cost only with vsync off and the scene genuinely GPU-bound, and compare back to back in one window.

## Navigation and persistent close-ups — 2026-09-22

- The previous camera had a narrow orbit, no pan and zoom along a fixed world axis. Dragging or scrolling also cancelled the snail close-up. Navigation now applies orbit, distance-scaled screen-plane pan and exponential dolly to the active subject. The orbit range is wider, zoom works in every preset, and snail visits stay open until an explicit subject/view change. Presets reset local framing; requesting the drop from the snail view opens the leaf study without inheriting the snail's tight zoom.
- Direct camera movement has one fast smoothing stage; preset transitions remain slower. Pointer parallax freezes after manual framing. Minimum distance, pitch limits and terrain clearance keep the camera above the ground, including during transitions. These are diorama bounds, not collision detection against every plant or log.
- Mouse drag or one touch orbits. Shift/right/middle drag pans; two touches pan and pinch. Wheel up approaches, wheel down retreats; trackpad pinch and keyboard zoom share the same path. Shift + arrows orbit and Alt + arrows pan without moving or enabling the carried light. Touch taps still place the light and activate the leaf, pool or snail.
- Drag distance is latched so returning to the original press position cannot trigger a click. Multi-touch sequences suppress accidental activation. Cancellation, lost capture and window blur clear the gesture. The on-page hints, Field notes and README describe the new controls.
- Production build and changed-file formatting checks passed. ## Remaining release checks

**The site is now live at https://hilimor.github.io/after-rain/ but has still only ever been run in Chrome on macOS.** Actual Safari/iOS and Android hardware, long-duration resource/thermal behavior, and a listening pass for synthesized sound have not been verified. Viewport emulation is not a phone: Safari's WebGPU behaviour, thermal throttling over several minutes, and mobile GPU performance remain unknown, and are the largest outstanding risk. No claim of a full accessibility audit or a universal frame-rate target is made. Further visual realism would benefit from finer moss, a more anatomical snail and contact shadows; mushroom silhouettes now vary by maturity, but the caps are still lathe surfaces rather than scanned fungi. See README for rendering and simulation limits.
